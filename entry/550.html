Methods Assay, extraction, and data processing text

Assay description

We performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on ETX embryoids generated with a novel optimized method established in this study.

Extraction protocol

ETX embryoids were collected on day 4, day 5 and day 6. ETX structures were selected on day 4 when they displayed a clear epiblast and a pro-amniotic cavity. Day-5 ETX embryoids were divided in a group displaying a morphology of early gastrulation and a group of ETX structures displaying signs of more advanced gastrulation. Selected day-6 ETX embryoids displayed a more developed phenotype with the apparent formation of an amnion, chorion and exocoelom. Per selected stage, 10 ETX structures were processed for 10x-sequencing as earlier described. Briefly, selected ETX embryoids were carefully removed from the wells and dissociated with TrypLE Express dissociation reagent (Life Technologies) at 37 °C for 5min and gently dissociated manually. Following inactivation with an equivalent amount of FBS, the single cells were filtered through a Flowmi Tip Strainer with 40 µm porosity (ThermoFisher Scientific, #136800040), washed and resuspended in PBS with 0.4% BSA. Single cells were processed on the 10X Genomics Chromium Platform using the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3′ Reagent kit v3.1 with dual indexing (10X Genomics) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, 15,000 cells were loaded in each channel of a chip to be partitioned into gel beads in emulsion (GEMs). Within GEMs, cells were lysed followed by barcoded reverse transcription of RNA. Breaking of the GEMs was followed by amplification, fragmentation and addition of adapter and sample index. Libraries were pooled and sequenced 28-10-10-90 cycles on an Illumina NovaSeq6000 instrument aiming for a minimum coverage of 25,000 raw reads per cell.

Main topic
This paper focuses on optimizing a method for generating ETX embryoids, which are stem cell-based models used to study early postimplantation development in mice, and demonstrates the reliable and efficient generation of developmentally advanced ETX embryoids that recapitulate murine egg cylinder development.
