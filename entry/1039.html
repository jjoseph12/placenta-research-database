Main topic
This paper investigates the similarities in DNA methylation patterns between the placenta and cancers, suggesting that common epigenetic mechanisms may regulate behaviors such as tissue invasion, angiogenesis, and immune system evasion. The study identifies megabase-scale patterns of hypomethylation in the early pregnancy placenta that mirror those in tumors, potentially leading to biomarkers and drug targets in oncology, obstetrics, and gynecology.

Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer The paper performs a genome-wide comparison of DNA methylation changes in human placental tissues during pregnancy and in solid tumors, identifying megabase-scale hypomethylated blocks shared between the early pregnancy placenta and cancer.
Source excerpt
We show here that megabase-scale patterns of hypomethylation distinguish first from third trimester chorionic villi in the placenta, and that these patterns mirror those that distinguish many tumors from corresponding normal tissues.

Reviewed answer First trimester chorionic villi (6-10 WG), third trimester chorionic villi (32-39 WG), early first trimester villous cytotrophoblasts (8-10 WG), and late first trimester villous cytotrophoblasts (12-14 WG).

First trimester chorionic villi samples were obtained between 6 and 10 weeks of gestation (WG), and third trimester chorionic villi samples were obtained between 32 and 39 WG. Early ﬁrst trimester villous cytotrophoblast samples were isolated from chorionic villi obtained before entrance of maternal blood into the intervillous space (8-10 WG), and late ﬁrst trimester villous cytotrophoblast samples were isolated from chorionic villi samples obtained after entrance of maternal blood into the intervillous space (12-14 WG).
