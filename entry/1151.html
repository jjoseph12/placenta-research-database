Methods Assay, extraction, and data processing text

Assay description

Five placentas were collected for study. Four placentas were dissociated for cell type sorting and bulk RNA-sequencing. Two placentas were dissociated for single-cell RNA-sequencing; each included a technical replicate. One placenta contributed data for both experiments.

Extraction protocol

Placentas were collected shortly after delivery from healthy, full term, singleton uncomplicated Cesarean sections at the University of Michigan Von Voigtlander Women’s Hospital. Villous placental tissue biopsies were collected and minced for dissociation after cutting away the basal and chorionic plates and scraping villous tissue from blood vessels. We subjected approximately 1g minced dissected villous tissue to the Miltenyi Tumor Dissociation Kit on the GentleMACS Octo Dissociator with Heaters (Miltenyi Biotec) to yield single-cell suspensions of viable placental cells in 5μM StemMACS™ Y27632 (Miltenyi Biotec) in RPMI 1640 (Gibco) according to manufacturer’s instructions for “soft” tumor type. Red blood cells were depleted using RBC lysis buffer (Biolegend) according to manufacturer’s protocol A. Single-cell suspensions were size-filtered at 100μm and subsequently 40μm. To collect a syncytiotrophoblast-enriched fraction, the fraction between 40μm and 100μm was washed from the 40μm strainers. Single-cell suspensions <40μm were cryogenically stored in 5μM StemMACS™ Y27632 90% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (Gibco)/10% dimethyl sulfoxide (Invitrogen). For each placenta, additional whole villous tissue samples were stored in RNALater (Qiagen). For single-cell RNA-sequencing, single cells were barcoded, and cDNA libraries constructed on the Chromium platform (10X Genomics, Single Cell 3' v2 chemistry). For bulk RNA-sequencing, ribosomal RNAs were depleted with RiboGone (Takara) and libraries were prepared with the SMARTer Stranded RNA-Seq v2 kit (Takara).

Main topic
This study generates a single-cell RNA-sequencing reference for placental villous tissue to understand placental cell type heterogeneity in preeclampsia and its impact on gene expression differences, finding that cell proportion adjustments attenuate gene expression differences between preeclampsia cases and controls.
