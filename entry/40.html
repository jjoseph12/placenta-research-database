Main topic
This study investigates the role of M1 macrophage-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) and their miRNA cargo (miR-146a/155) in triggering epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in amniotic epithelial cells (AECs), leading to premature rupture of membranes (PROM) in mice and humans.

Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer The study investigates the mechanism by which extracellular vesicles (EVs) derived from M1 macrophages in the amniotic fluid trigger the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of amniotic epithelial cells, leading to a decline in the biomechanical properties of the amniotic membrane and resulting in premature rupture of membranes (PROM).
Source excerpt
This study revealed that under inflammatory conditions, EV-miR-146a/155 from M1 macrophages could trigger EMTs and MMP-9 transcription in AECs, elevating the risk of PROM in both mice and humans.
