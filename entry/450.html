Main topic
This paper reports a bioengineering-inspired approach to create embryoids by coaxing mouse embryonic stem cells into epiblast-like aggregates and co-culturing them with mouse trophoblast stem cell aggregates, resulting in structures that undergo gastrulation-like events and axial morphogenesis.

Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer The development of an in vitro bioengineered embryoid model, termed EpiTS embryoids, using mouse embryonic stem cells and trophoblast stem cells to model early post-implantation development, gastrulation, and axial morphogenesis.
Source excerpt
Here, we report a bioengineering-inspired approach aimed at addressing this gap. We employ a high-throughput cell aggregation approach to simultaneously coax mouse embryonic stem cells into hundreds of uniform epiblast-like aggregates... When co-cultured with mouse trophoblast stem cell aggregates, the resulting hybrid structures initiate gastrulation-like events and undergo axial morphogenesis to yield structures, termed EpiTS embryoids
