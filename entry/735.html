Methods text

Assay description

We employed covalent derivatization of CG sites combined with lower cost next generation sequencing, or quantitative PCR for analysis of fetal chorionic villi tissue samples, and cfDNA of pregnant and non-pregnant women.

Extraction protocol

Chorionic villi gDNA was purified using standard phenol-chloroform extraction. Cell-free DNA was isolated from blood plasma with "QIAamp MinElute ccfDNA kit" according to the manufacturer‘s instructions. Libraries were constructed according to TOP-seq (Stasevskij et al., 2017) and hmTOP-seq (Gibas et al., 2020) protocols. Briefly, cfDNA and sonicated gDNA were labeled with azide moieties using Ado-6-azide (for uCG analysis) or UDP-6-azide-glucose (for 5hmCG analysis) cofactors. After ligation of adapters, the alkyne-bearing oligodeoxyribonucleotides (ODNs) with (5hmCG) or without (uCG) biotin were attached to the azide-modified DNA. Biotinylated DNA was enriched with streptavidin-coupled magnetic beads. Purified DNA after ODN conjugation (uCG) or biotin-enrichment (5hmC) was subsequently used in a DNA priming reaction. The products were amplified, size-selected and subjected to Ion Proton sequencing.

Main topic
This paper proposes that targeting either unmodified or 5-hydroxymethylated CG sites specifically enriches fetal genetic material and reduces the number of required analytical sequencing reads, thereby decreasing the cost of non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT). The study demonstrates that uTOP-seq and hmTOP-seq approaches provide a cost-efficient and sensitive epigenetic analysis of fetal abnormalities in maternal cfDNA.
