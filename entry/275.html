Methods text

Assay description

Profiling of DNA methylation in trophoblast stem cells in normal conditions and treated with DNMT1 or EZH2 inhibitor.

Extraction protocol

The DNA was extracted using the PureLink Genomic DNA Mini Kit (Thermo, #K182002) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Concentration of genomic DNA (gDNA) was quantified using a Qubit 3.0 Fluorometer. RRBS was performed on 100 ng gDNA of each sample using the NuGen Ovation® RRBS Methyl-Seq System (Tecan, #0353) following the manufacturer’s recommendations with the following modifications (6). After the final repair step, the bisulfite conversion of DNA was conducted using the Qiagen EpiTect Fast Bisulfite Conversion kit (Qiagen, #59824) following the manufacturer’s recommendations, eluting the bisulfite converted DNA in 23 µl EB. Libraries were amplified with 12 cycles of PCR. Amplified library purification with Agencourt RNAclean XP beads (Beckman Coulter, #A63987) was performed twice (1X). The purified libraries were quality-assessed on an Agilent 4150 Tape-Station HS D1000 ScreenTape and sequenced for 100-bp single-end reads on the NovaSeq6000 (Illumina).

Main topic
This paper investigates the dynamic antagonism between DNA methyltransferase and Polycomb activity in maintaining the placental epigenome, particularly focusing on the intermediate methylation levels observed in trophoblast stem cells.
Workbook excerpt Source evidence 31 evidence notes linked 0 supplement repair findings
Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer The publication investigates the epigenetic regulation of the mammalian extra-embryonic lineage, showing that its intermediate DNA methylation state is maintained by a dynamic, antagonistic relationship between de novo DNA methyltransferases (specifically DNMT3B) and the Polycomb repressive complexes.
Source excerpt
We find that the extra-embryonic epigenome reflects ongoing and dynamic de novo methyltransferase recruitment, which is continuously antagonized by Polycomb to maintain intermediate, locally disordered methylation.
