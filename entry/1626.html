G9a histone methyltransferase maintains genomic imprinting in the mouse placenta.

Study details Samples, assay, platform, and submission

Sample characteristics

  • Strain: The G9a gene was trapped by insertion of a beta-galactosidase-neomycin phosphotransferase (beta-geo) construct comprising a splice acceptor (Sutherland et al. 2001). We used a G9a-trapped ES cell line, ES62, to generate chimaeric mice by blastocyst injection. Germ line transmission was obtained from these chimaeras. The obtained transgenic line was maintained in a hemizygous state and was crossed for four generations to C57BL/6 mice. Concomitantly, a second line was derived by back-crossing to a (M. spretus) congenic mouse line, SDP711. G9a-/- embryos and placentas were obtained by inter-crossing these two lines.
  • Age: 9.5 dpc.
  • Tissue: placenta.
  • Strain: C57BL/6.

Methods Assay, extraction, and data processing text

Assay description

Number of samples: four (two biologically replicate G9a-/- pooled placentae samples, and two biologically replicate wildtype pooled placentae samples). The first G9a-/- and wildtype replicates were used to hybridize Affymetrix MOE430A and MOE430B arrays (four arrays total). The second replicates were used to hybridize Affymetrix Mouse430_2 arrays (two arrays total).

Main topic
This paper investigates the role of G9a histone methyltransferase in genomic imprinting in the mouse placenta, focusing on histone H3-K9 methylation at imprinting control regions (ICRs).
