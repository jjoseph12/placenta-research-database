Methods Assay, extraction, and data processing text

Assay description

Porcine trophoblast primary cells were treated in vitro with the uterine-derived EVs collected from the same female on day 16 of pregnancy. Then, comparative gene expression profiling analysis of RNA-seq data for porcine primary trophoblast cells was performed.

Extraction protocol

Total RNA was isolated using miRVana miRNA isolation kit and stored at -80°C in RNase-free H2O until assayed. Quantity and quality of RNA were assessed using Bioanalyzer 2100 and Qubit 3.0 Fluorometer. One microgram of total RNA was used to construct cDNA libraries with the TruSeq Stranded Total RNA with Ribo-Zero Gold Human/Mouse/Rat kit following manufacturer's protocols.

Main topic
This paper investigates the role of extracellular vesicles (EVs) in the communication between the embryo and the endometrium during the early stages of pregnancy, focusing on how EVs and their miRNA cargo affect trophoblast cell physiology in a porcine model.

Reviewed answer The study demonstrates that early pregnancy in the pig is associated with the release of extracellular vesicles (EVs) in the uterine lumen, which carry miRNAs that are internalized by trophoblast cells and regulate early implantation processes such as migration, invasion, and proliferation.
Source excerpt
Here, for the first time, we show that the uterine cavity during early pregnancy is abundant in EVs containing numerous miRNAs that can be internalized by porcine trophoblast cells, wherein they affect gene expression, stimulate proliferation, and inhibit migration and invasion.
