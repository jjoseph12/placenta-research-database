Main topic
This paper investigates the role of APOBEC3A, an intrinsic cellular restriction factor, in the maternal-fetal interface as an innate immune barrier against human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) transmission, which is a leading cause of congenital infection.

Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer The study investigates the upregulation of the intrinsic cellular restriction factor APOBEC3A (A3A) by human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) in the maternal-fetal interface and demonstrates its role as an innate anti-HCMV effector.
Source excerpt
Here we show that the intrinsic cellular restriction factor apo lipoprotein B editing catalytic subunit-like 3A (APOBEC3A [A3A]) is profoundly upregulated following ex vivo HCMV infection in human decidual tissues—constituting the maternal aspect of the placenta.
