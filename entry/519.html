Methods text

Assay description

Examination of the impact of EED deletion in oocytes on placental transcription and offspring development

Extraction protocol

Placentas were isolated at E17.5, weighed and bisected. Half of the placenta was rinsed in PBS, snap-frozen on dry ice and stored at -80°C for RNA analysis. Growth plate and placental RNA was extracted from 4-5 samples / genotype using NucleoSpin RNA Plus columns. RNA quality was assessed on an Agilent Bioanalyser and samples with RIN >7.5 used for library preparation and sequencing on the BGI Genomics platform (placentae, BGI Genomics, Hong Kong). Placental libraries were constructed using the BGI DNBseq protocol followed by 150bp PE sequencing on the BGI DNBSeq Eukaryotic Transcriptome Resequencing platform (Beijing Genomics) Placentas were isolated at E17.5, weighed and bisected. Half of the placenta was rinsed in PBS, snap-frozen on dry ice and stored at -80°C for RRBS analysis. DNA was extracted from 5-6 samples / genotype using Qiagen DNAeasy columns. DNA quality was assessed on agaose gels and samples sent to CD-Genomics for library preparation and sequencing. Genomic DNA was quantified using the Qubit High Sensitivity assay (Life Technologies). 1 µg of genomic DNA was digested with MspI (NEB), followed by ends preparation, adaptor ligation using Premium RRBS kit (Diagenode). Size selection was performed using AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter, Inc.) to obtain DNA fractions of MspI-digested products enriching for the most CpG-rich regions in the range of 150-350 bp. Subsequently, bisulfite treatment was conducted using the ZYMO EZ DNA Methylation-Gold Kit. The converted DNAs were then amplified by twelve cycles of PCR, using 25 μl KAPA HiFi HotStart Uracil+ ReadyMix (2X) and 8-bp index primers with a final concentration of 1 μM each and clean-up using AMPure XP beads. The constructed RRBS libraries were quantified by a Qubit fluorometer with Quant-iT dsDNA HS Assay Kit (Invitrogen), and were sequenced on Illumina Novaseq6000 platform using paired-end 150 bp strategy in CD Genomics (Shirley, NY)

Main topic
This study investigates the impact of loss of the PRC2 protein Embryonic Ectoderm Development (EED) in mouse oocytes on offspring development, showing an initial developmental delay followed by accelerated fetal development and growth in late gestation, resulting in offspring with similar weight to controls at birth.

Workbook field Main topic of the publication
Reviewed answer This publication investigates how the loss of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) protein EED in mouse oocytes causes mid-gestation developmental delay and fetal growth restriction, which is subsequently resolved by late gestational growth acceleration and leads to offspring overgrowth.
Source excerpt
In this study, we show that offspring from mouse oocytes lacking the PRC2 protein Embryonic Ectoderm Development (EED) were initially developmentally delayed, characterised by low blastocyst cell counts and substantial growth delay in mid-gestation embryos. This initial developmental delay was resolved as offspring underwent accelerated fetal development and growth in late gestation resulting in offspring that were similar stage and weight to controls at birth.
